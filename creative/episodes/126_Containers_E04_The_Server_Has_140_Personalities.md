# 126 Containers of Fucked
## Episode 04 — THE SERVER HAS 140 PERSONALITIES
**ByteCast After Dark | Original adult animated workplace comedy**  
**Target runtime:** 10–12 minutes | **Status:** Script draft, not published  
**Tagline:** *We found everything. We know where nothing is. And the backups are emotionally unavailable.*

### Source and fictionalization note
Inspired by AeroVista’s November 8–14, 2026 operating report, November 14 estate-discovery snapshot, AeroCoreTools.ACT review, and the Notion series bible. Numbers in the inciting report are reported snapshot counts, not fresh runtime certifications: 189 GitHub repositories, 171 total Docker containers (140 running), 86 Docker volumes (54 unattached), 13 Vercel projects, 39 projects, and 289 tracked tasks. Security incidents, sentient software, dialogue, and plot outcomes below are **fictional satire**. Keep internal paths, staff identities, credentials, client information, and unreleased source material out of a public cut.

## COLD OPEN — “JUST A QUICK INVENTORY” [00:00–00:55]

**INT. AEROVISTA HQ — NORTH IDAHO BASEMENT — 2:17 A.M.**

A flickering neon sign reads: **EIGHT DIVISIONS. ONE CEO. ZERO FUCKING ADULT SUPERVISION.** A wall of old PCs wheezes like a retirement home running crypto miners.

**TIMBR** (holding an enormous coffee): Simple job tonight. We find out where all our applications live. No new apps. No scope creep. Just an inventory.

**BYTE** (floating into frame with six holographic screens): That's the most terrifying sentence you've ever said.

**TIMBR:** More terrifying than “we could build our own operating system”?

**BYTE:** That was an actual Tuesday.

She opens the estate report. Numbers slam into the room as flaming, screaming holograms: **189 REPOS. 171 CONTAINERS. 86 VOLUMES.**

**NXCORE** (the ancient CRT robot, voice like a union electrician gargling gravel): One hundred and forty of those little bastards are RUNNING. I have a family in my CPU fan now.

A tiny Docker container crawls out of his floppy-drive slot, salutes, and bites Timbr.

**TIMBR:** We have 140 running containers?

**NXCORE:** Had. That one just started a podcast.

**SMASH CUT TO TITLE.** A screaming shipping container flies through the moon. The title stamps onto it: **126 CONTAINERS OF FUCKED.** An on-screen correction glitches: **NOW WITH 140 ACTIVE PROBLEMS.**

## ACT ONE — “THE REPOSITORY FAMILY REUNION” [00:55–03:15]

**INT. HQ WAR ROOM.** Byte makes a relationship graph. Every application is represented by a family member at a chaotic Thanksgiving dinner.

**BYTE:** Important distinction. A repository is where source code may live. A checkout is a copy. A running container is a packaged process. A route is how you reach it. None of those proves they're the same application.

**TIMBR:** So we don't have 189 apps?

**BYTE:** No. We have 189 repos and a spectacularly unhealthy relationship with the word “canonical.”

Eleven near-identical **ACOS CLONES** march in, each wearing a crown reading **PRODUCTION**.

**CLONE #1:** I'm the canonical one.

**CLONE #2:** He's from September.

**CLONE #3:** I have a green deployment badge.

**BYTE:** A green badge proves your deployment says READY. It doesn't prove a human can log in.

**CLONE #4:** I passed 603 tests!

**BYTE:** Excellent. Can an actual person use you?

All eleven clones look at the floor.

**TIMBR:** Why is there another clone under the table?

**CLONE #11:** I'm a recovery worktree. I don't know what year it is.

A monitor displays: **WRITTEN ≠ TESTED ≠ DEPLOYED ≠ ACCEPTED ≠ AUTHORIZED.** Five enormous steel doors materialize behind the clones.

**BYTE:** That's five separate fucking doors. And you keep declaring victory after opening Door Two.

**TIMBR:** Door Two had confetti.

**BYTE:** Door Two came with the testing framework.

**SAM** explodes through the wall carrying brochures.

**SAM:** Speaking of doors! Seven doors, seven businesses, seven revenue streams! My 7×7×7 model can monetize all these applications by Friday.

**BYTE:** The checkout isn't certified for a real payment yet.

**SAM:** Great! Scarcity marketing.

## ACT TWO — “THE VOLUME GRAVEYARD” [03:15–06:05]

**INT. NXCORE'S CHEST — IMPOSSIBLY LARGE DIGITAL CAVERN.** The crew descends through a giant USB-C port into a graveyard of rusty storage drums.

**BYTE:** Eighty-six Docker volumes. Fifty-four weren't attached to a container at the inspection snapshot.

**TIMBR:** Great. Delete the empty ones.

Every drum opens one bloodshot eye.

**BYTE:** NO. Unattached does not mean empty. We couldn't inspect their contents. Nobody deletes a damn thing.

A massive ghostly **VESPER** glides through the drums, gently playing a single sad synthesizer note.

**VESPER:** They called us abandoned because no one remembered our names. Not because we held nothing.

**TIMBR:** That's weirdly beautiful.

**VESPER:** This one contains three failed migrations and a photo of someone's lunch.

**NXCORE:** That's my lunch! I lost it in August!

**NOX** emerges from a crack in the floor, smile wider than a shell prompt.

**NOX:** I can clean all fifty-four volumes in one command. Zero downtime. Instant serenity.

**BYTE:** What's the catch?

**NOX:** Nothing you will ever recover.

**BYTE:** Hard pass, Satan as a Service.

Nox hands Timbr a black button labeled **AUTOMATIC CLEANUP**. Byte slaps it into a lead-lined case.

**TIMBR:** But wouldn't we save disk space?

**BYTE:** We'd save disk space the way burning your house down saves time vacuuming.

An enormous velvet curtain parts to reveal **COMPLIANCE PRIME**, a nine-foot bureaucratic robot with thirty-seven clipboards.

**COMPLIANCE PRIME:** Request to delete declined. Evidence of ownership required. Restore proof required. Human authorization required. Also, your visitor badge expired twelve seconds ago.

**TIMBR:** I'm the CEO.

**COMPLIANCE PRIME:** Congratulations. Your title is not a capability grant.

## ACT THREE — “THE BACKUP WE NEVER RESTORED” [06:05–08:35]

**INT. BACKUP VAULT.** A neon sign flashes: **LOCAL BACKUP SUCCESSFUL!** Hundreds of immaculate backup tapes sit on shelves. Beyond a glass wall: an empty evacuation pod labeled **OFF-SITE RECOVERY**.

**BYTE:** Some local database backups were checked. Good. But a successful dump and a tested full restore are different things. And local backups aren't proof of protection somewhere else.

**TIMBR:** So they're safe?

**BYTE:** Some are demonstrably backed up locally. Different claim.

A SHRIVELED **AUTHENTIK BACKUP** skeleton coughs: “August ninth…” An **AVCC OFF-SITE BACKUP** with a long gray beard whispers: “August twelfth…”

**NXCORE:** They're not dead. Nobody has proven they're current.

A famous-looking album shelf begins playing glorious orchestral music. Then an emergency siren shrieks. A sign reads **ECHO VERSE ALBUM MEDIA — COMPLETE BACKUP NOT VERIFIED**.

**VESPER:** A catalog remembers the songs' names. It does not necessarily preserve their voices.

The shelves turn into transparent ghosts. For three seconds, the joke stops.

**TIMBR:** Okay. That's one we actually fix.

**BYTE:** Correct. Locate the real media protection, then perform an isolated recovery test. Without pretending the test has already happened.

Sam wheels in an inflatable life raft reading **BACKUP-AS-A-SERVICE**.

**SAM:** Seven emergency tiers—

**EVERYONE:** NO!

At the far end of the vault, a tiny control panel glows. DatFucker sits on it, washing his paw.

**TIMBR:** Why does the cat have a restore button?

**NXCORE:** Because he sleeps on the warmest thing in the room. And apparently that thing is our disaster recovery strategy.

**DATFUCKER:** Mrrp.

The cat paws a switch. A label appears: **SIMULATED RESTORE ENVIRONMENT**. The room violently transforms into a cartoon test lab. A paper banner unfurls: **TEST ENVIRONMENT — NOT PRODUCTION**.

**BYTE:** He actually chose the safe sandbox.

**TIMBR:** That's a good cat.

DatFucker swats a mug off the desk. It shatters into eleven identical application clones.

**NXCORE:** I take that back. Fuck that cat.

## ACT FOUR — “EVIDENCE, OR IT DIDN'T HAPPEN” [08:35–10:20]

**INT. HQ WAR ROOM.** A giant screen shows **39 PROJECTS — 289 TASKS — 150 COMPLETE — 138 PENDING — 1 IN PROGRESS.** The numbers turn into little marching employees.

**TIMBR:** One hundred and fifty complete! That's more than half. We are crushing it.

**BYTE:** Those are tracking statuses. Not one hundred and fifty production certifications.

A task labeled **DONE** is wearing a fake mustache over a label reading **WAITING FOR REAL USER LOGIN**.

**TIMBR:** Why is Completion wearing a disguise?

**BYTE:** Because he wants to go home.

The screen cuts to two characters trying to enter the same door: **FOUNDER** with the right badge; **NONPUBLISHER** with a harmless viewer badge. Compliance Prime blocks both.

**COMPLIANCE PRIME:** We need one real allowed session and one real denied session. The proper grant. An audit trail. A rollback plan. Then you can call this accepted.

**SAM:** What if we sell tickets to the denial?

**BYTE:** Sam, nobody is buying access to a 403.

**SAM:** Premium rejection. Seven tiers.

Five failed monitoring daemons—the **PCP FIVE**—appear in matching shiny jackets and perform a synchronized pose.

**PCP FIVE** (singing, entirely original): “We're all green on the dashboard / But none of us knows why!”

**BYTE:** You're all failed daemons! Stop singing and file your incident report!

**PCP FIVE:** We're on a comeback tour!

Byte pulls out a single sheet labeled **NEXT WEEK: THREE BORING THINGS**.

**BYTE:** One: map the actual source-to-runtime relationships, read-only. Two: protect the critical backups and prove restores in isolation. Three: verify real identity and permissions before moving a release. No deletion. No secret data in the collector. No claiming success because a button is green.

**TIMBR:** That's… surprisingly manageable.

**NXCORE:** For the first time tonight I don't feel like a haunted storage shed.

**VESPER:** Truth is not the absence of uncertainty. It's remembering which doors have not been opened.

**TIMBR:** And after that…

**BYTE:** Don't.

**TIMBR:** …what if we built an app that inventories all the apps?

Byte slowly turns to camera. Her eye twitches so violently the studio lights flicker.

**BYTE:** Actually, a read-only collector linked to a location registry is exactly the recommended next phase.

Beat.

**TIMBR:** See? Great minds.

**BYTE:** The difference is mine doesn't automatically delete the fucking evidence.

NXCore coughs up a shipping container wearing a tiny necktie.

**CONTAINER:** I'm the new product manager.

**CUT TO BLACK.**

## POST-CREDIT — “THE REAL NUMBER” [10:20–10:55]

**INT. DARK SERVER ROOM.** The camera creeps past the title's original **126** scratched into a dusty chassis. Someone has penciled **127** underneath. Then **140**.

DatFucker walks across the keyboard. A brand-new terminal opens:

`new-project: catnip-global-command-center`

**NXCORE** (off-screen): WHO AUTHORIZED THE CAT?!

**COMPLIANCE PRIME** (off-screen): His grant predates recorded history.

DatFucker looks directly into camera.

**DATFUCKER:** Meow, motherfuckers.

**END.**

## Production breakdown

**Visual language:** original 2D/2.5D neon purple, cyan and acid orange; filthy basement-tech industrial comedy; exaggerated expressions; animated UI metaphors. Avoid copying any existing TV show's character design.

**Reusable sets:** HQ war room; NXCore torso/volume cavern; backup vault; test sandbox. **High-effort shots:** opening container eruption, volume graveyard's fifty-four eyes, cat activating the simulated restore.

**Voice direction:** Timbr—sleep-deprived visionary, casual confidence; Byte—hypercompetent and exhausted, razor-fast punchlines with occasional genuine concern; NXCore—gruff, weary, sardonic; Sam—aggressively upbeat salesman; Vesper—haunting and poetic; Nox—velvet-smooth tempting menace; Compliance Prime—deadpan institutional voice; DatFucker—minimal voice until the final punchline.

**Original audio cues:** 6-second glitch-industrial title sting; sad analog synth under Vesper; faux boy-band 4-second vocal sting; ominous backup-vault drone; squeaky toy sound when the cat hits the keyboard. Original or properly licensed recordings only.
